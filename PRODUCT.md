# War Chest
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
Native desktop game (Godot; macOS first, portable to Windows/Linux). This platform is outside the web/mobile-only Impeccable enum; do not misclassify it as a website.

## Users
Sarthak, playing on a computer for personal enjoyment. No commercial audience or monetization requirements.

## Product Purpose
War Chest helps a strategy-game lover grow an enduring warband through tactical battles and spatial equipment builds.

## Operating Context
Single-player, mouse and keyboard, fully turn-based combat. Short resumable sessions. Local main branch and private repository were explicitly requested.

## Capabilities and Constraints
The owner wants levels, character growth, skills, equipment upgrades, heroes, pets, packing puzzles, cubes and defense. Earned-gold merchants are the stated interpretation of buying; no real-money purchase systems. Every company level must unlock actual playable content. Current slice has six company levels, six main encounters and six authored side quests, not a finished long campaign. The whole window must be the game, with compact HUD overlays rather than an app dashboard; 2D is acceptable and 3D is not a requirement.

## Evidence on Hand
Source-based research of 29 games. Owner selected B: Iron & Ember's three-front warfare and equipment-driven commands, then rejected realistic detailed art. Owner selected cartoon B: Pocket Siege, rejected the crude geometric implementation, and on September 23 agreed to retry the selected direction with illustrated characters, proper terrain, larger cards and quieter controls. Current work is a battle-screen trial; the owner has not accepted the result or the other screens. No hands-on retention evidence yet.

## Product Principles
Current expansion preserves the selected visual system and adds three repeatable contract types with five renown tiers, six persistent rankable banners and differentiated enemy tactics. Six company levels remain the authored level cap. The owner requested more gameplay; this is not acceptance of final visual quality or evidence of long-term enjoyment.

The journey expansion adds rescue, convoy, protected relic recovery, watchfire, iron patrol and siege-engine side quests across three chapters. Main-campaign victories and earlier quests gate these stories; quest milestones alternatively open contract tiers II–V without mandatory renown grinding. Three six-cell relics bring the equipment catalog to 43 cells competing for a 30-cell chest. Later main fights and the final two quests telegraph rotating bombardments. Old active saves retain their existing rules. Tests prove a legal earned route through all twelve encounters, not human fun or balanced long-term retention.

- Preparation changes tactical options; combat remains under the player's control.
- Progress belongs to the player, including after defeat.
- Visible threats and reliable rules outweigh random spectacle.
- Daily enjoyment, never daily obligation.

## Open Decisions
Owner confirmed the earlier native Practice launch, rejected its childish presentation, and explicitly selected refined A — Banner & Steel. The running source now uses mature flat illustrated troops, olive/stone colors, three vertical approaches and restrained native controls. New campaigns enter the actual first defense, earn an unpacked storm rune, learn edge-adjacent equipment boosting and test it in battle two. Banner choice and guided discovery of broader systems follow the second victory; skipping opens free exploration. Legacy saves are never enrolled or reset. Headless legal-action and UI tests cover the loop; native rendering, background stability and subjective enjoyment remain unqualified. A self-contained local app wrapper exists; no release is published.

Individual hero mastery now continues through nine wins per deployed hero, independent of the six company levels. Four command synergies and optional bonus objectives add tactical goals; neither is a claim of unlimited content or proven retention.

Long-campaign size, final name, additional biomes and release packaging remain uncommitted. Small action economy, item counts and reward values are prototype balance hypotheses.
