## Platform
Native desktop game (Godot; macOS first, portable to Windows/Linux). This platform is outside the web/mobile-only Impeccable enum; do not misclassify it as a website.

## Users
Sarthak, playing on a computer for personal enjoyment. Potential paying players are now a quality consideration: the owner doubts the current game would satisfy them. No price, storefront, payment system or commercial release has been authorized.

## Product Purpose
War Chest helps a strategy-game lover grow an enduring warband through an authored story, tactical battles and spatial equipment builds.

## Operating Context
Single-player, mouse and keyboard, fully turn-based combat. Short resumable sessions. Local main branch and private repository were explicitly requested.

## Capabilities and Constraints
The owner wants levels, character growth, skills, equipment upgrades, heroes, pets, packing puzzles, cubes and defense. Earned-gold merchants are the stated interpretation of buying; no real-money purchase systems. Every company level must unlock actual playable content. Story mode is a finite three-chapter campaign spanning six main encounters and six authored quests, with six paced company levels and a homecoming. Legacy free exploration remains available separately. The whole window must be the game, with compact HUD overlays rather than an app dashboard; 2D is acceptable and 3D is not a requirement.

## Product Principles

The owner now requests a story-mode game and says the game must make players care before charging. The implemented story is a complete three-chapter, twelve-encounter authored campaign about returning the valley's stolen winter wages. Named relationships, practical choices with battle/ending consequences, rescue and extraction give the existing tactics a reason. `STORY.md` and `.fleet/story-mode-plan.md` define the scope. The owner selected A — Company & Consequences after three rendered native comparisons. Fresh companies begin with Lysa's missing brother and the families outside Lantern Gate. The original company remains in its separate save; the story can be entered explicitly without resetting it.

Both authored routes are earned and exactly replayable using public game actions. Native input has played the opening conversation, Gate victory, aftermath, Ashen briefing, rune packing and the real 10-damage kill payoff. Fixture rendering and callback checks additionally cover choices, defeat, ending, banner equipment and save-slot recovery. This proves implementation and preserves player progress; it does not establish emotional attachment, replay value or buyer satisfaction.

Onboarding must teach that the whole company deploys and the player selects who receives orders, explain free story recruitment separately from legacy purchases, and distinguish automatic company-level milestones from individual mastery. In-story Merrin joins after the Sunken Reliquary with his staff in storage; commands from equipment require actual packing. Every level reward must name what is ready next battle and what needs a purchase, talent choice or packing. This teaching preserves the selected presentation and existing optional field lessons.
Current expansion preserves the selected visual system and adds three repeatable contract types with five renown tiers, six persistent rankable banners and differentiated enemy tactics. Six company levels remain the authored level cap. The owner requested more gameplay; this is not acceptance of final visual quality or evidence of long-term enjoyment.

The journey expansion adds rescue, convoy, protected relic recovery, watchfire, iron patrol and siege-engine side quests across three chapters. Main-campaign victories and earlier quests gate these stories; quest milestones alternatively open contract tiers II–V without mandatory renown grinding. Three six-cell relics bring the equipment catalog to 43 cells competing for a 30-cell chest. Later main fights and the final two quests telegraph rotating bombardments. Old active saves retain their existing rules. Tests prove a legal earned route through all twelve encounters, not human fun or balanced long-term retention.

- Preparation changes tactical options; combat remains under the player's control.
- Progress belongs to the player, including after defeat.
- Visible threats and reliable rules outweigh random spectacle.
- Daily enjoyment, never daily obligation.
