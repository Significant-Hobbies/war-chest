# Story campaign: the winter wages

Playable story mode, October 2. Authored dialogue and choices live in `scripts/story_data.gd`; deterministic state and consequences live in `scripts/story_game.gd`. The owner selected A — Company & Consequences for the native scenes, preparation and campaign road. Existing saved campaigns keep their original company; story uses a separate local save slot.

## What the player should care about

Start with Lysa's missing brother and families outside a shut gate. The player has a company that can open it. The cause of the war is revealed through what the company does for those people, rather than a lore lecture before combat.

The stolen war chest holds ordinary households' winter wages. Rowan's old signature helped turn those wages into siege engines. Recovering the chest makes his responsibility concrete: bring back what he authorized taking, and give the survivors a home rather than another order.

Equipment uses the company's separate earned gold. The missing wages remain in the sealed pay chest; the quartermaster makes this distinction visible so repairing a sword does not imply spending the money the company promised to return.

There is no chosen-one prophecy, permanent hero death, paid currency or daily obligation. Defeat means the company withdraws wounded and must try its promise again. The story acknowledges earned choices; it does not invent player attachment from test completion.

## Company

- **Rowan:** a veteran quartermaster, practiced at reading supplies as totals. He starts by admitting he signed the order to shut the gate. By the ending he reads the names and lets the households keep the ledger.
- **Lysa:** a border scout. Her first priority is Ivo, not the kingdom. After his rescue, she commits to the other families whose names are in the chest. Her skepticism toward Rowan changes through work done together.
- **Fen:** Lysa's wolf; treated as a companion with small everyday habits, not a speaking mascot. He follows Ivo's scent and has to be reminded not to chase during the convoy.
- **Ivo:** Lysa's brother, the scout who counted the carts and knows their contents. He stays behind to distribute supplies and later sends help to Winterwatch. The company sees the value of its earlier rescue return.
- **Merrin:** the keeper of a second inventory in a flooded archive. He joins after the company helps recover it. His runes can shelter people or expose an enemy; the player decides their use.
- **The Crown:** the authority that treated households as a sum to transfer. His defeat alone does not end the game. The chest still has to be carried out and returned.

## Decisions the game must remember

The choices are practical disagreements about how to protect people. Avoid an obvious virtuous option versus a selfish reward button.

1. **Cart:** use recovered steel to reinforce it (30 cart health), or build a decoy (25 cart health and a different initial enemy formation). The supplies are delivered either way; their reuse is acknowledged at home.
2. **Seal:** start the marshal encounter with four extra block on each hero, or deal two extra damage to the marshal. The ending returns that knowledge to peaceful work.
3. **Winterwatch:** defend six turns with 30 gate health, or evacuate in four with 20 gate health and Rowan protecting the Lower gate. Holding permits return to the keep; evacuating leads to new homes below Lantern Gate.

Each option must be winnable through earned gear and legal actions. Explain tactical consequences before commitment. Store decisions once, snapshot them into active battles, and reflect them in dialogue/journal/ending. Skipping dialogue cannot secretly choose an option.

## Pacing and continuity

Company levels advance after Gate, Convoy, Watchfires, Iron Oath and Winterwatch. Other victories still award earned gold, equipment, mastery and banners but do not rush the level cap. No contract grinding is required. Merrin and his staff are unavailable before the archive recovery; he joins there without resetting an already-owned staff rank.

Keep intro and outro conversations short. The next task always names who needs help and what success requires. Show a finite road with three chapters, not an endless list of contracts. Preparation remains the spatial chest; dialogue must not swallow combat or hide the difference between stored and deployed equipment.

The company road exposes earned banner equipment and a journal of completed promises and decisions. Merrin joins only after the archive recovery; Fen stays throughout the authored journey. Contracts and Talon open after the epilogue. Company levels advance at five deliberate milestones, rather than reaching the cap halfway along the road.

## Evidence needed

Legal route tests establish that both sets of choices can reach the ending, preserve saves and award once. Matched omission tests establish why rescue, convoy, signals, recovery and escape need different plans. Native first-play inspection establishes readable story controls and understandable stakes. Fresh human play is still needed to tell us whether the cast is memorable and whether someone wants another chapter without prompting.
